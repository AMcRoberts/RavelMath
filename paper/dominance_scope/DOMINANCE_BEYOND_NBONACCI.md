# Positive-word dominance in n-bonacci carry systems: arithmetic construction, Q/R reduction, finite twists, and proof-producing reflection

**Draft for circulation**  
**Author and contribution metadata:** to be finalized from `AUTHORS.md` and `CITATION.cff` before submission.

## Abstract

We study a dominance phenomenon arising from finite-state carry systems associated with the n-bonacci family. The development begins with a number-theoretic construction: an exact integral recurrence for carry states, its characteristic matrix, and a dimension-parametric determinant identity. The latter was generated by a reflective C++ proof pipeline and accepted by the Lean kernel for every dimension parameter `n >= 2`. Exact finite arithmetic certificates then isolate a recurrent component with maximal Perron growth in the tested n-bonacci carry graphs.

The decisive structural step is a passage from a single adjacency matrix to a canonical ordered pair `(Q,R)`. Here `Q` records balanced parent-prefix transport and `R` records one-sided prefix-defect transport. The dimension extension is described not by a commutative scalar recurrence but by a finite nonnegative polynomial in noncommuting Q/R words. A simultaneous intertwining inequality for `Q` and `R` therefore propagates through every word and every finite nonnegative word polynomial. The abstract word and polynomial propagation, the dimension-induction interface, and the final order sandwich have been kernel checked in Lean. Executable bridge certificates identify the concrete Condition-F quotient, its boundary substitution, and its literal carry realization with this abstract comparison pattern.

Finite sheet, orientation, phase, and holonomy data appear naturally as twists of the Q/R parent system. Channelized permutation lifts preserve unique path lifting and hence the positive Perron growth of the base system; Fourier or representation sectors are modulus-dominated by the trivial positive sector. Products and gauge changes of such twists remain in the same regime. This exposes both the breadth and the natural limit of the theorem. Contractive operator twists remain dominated by the unweighted channel-count system, while arbitrary expansive twists do not: the one-loop scalar twist `[2]` already exceeds the unweighted base `[1]`. The resulting counterexample points toward a norm-weighted Q/R majorant theorem in which edge-operator norms replace channel counts.

The contribution is thus both mathematical and methodological: a positive-word dominance principle tailored to a recursively generated n-bonacci/Condition-F family, together with a proof-producing reflection architecture in which ordinary exact C++ computations emit typed provenance, reusable derivation operations construct certificates, and Lean checks the serialized proof. We state carefully which results are kernel checked, which are executable bridge certificates, and which belong to the proposed norm-weighted extension.

## 1. Introduction

Dominance questions for substitution, numeration, and carry automata often begin in an apparently familiar form. A finite directed graph has several recurrent components; one expects a geometrically or arithmetically distinguished component to carry the maximal exponential path growth. If all relevant matrices were fixed nonnegative matrices, classical Perron--Frobenius comparison would supply much of the needed language: entrywise order propagates to powers and therefore to spectral radii [@BermanPlemmons1994; @Seneta2006]. The systems considered here are more structured and more difficult for three reasons.

First, the graph itself depends on the n-bonacci dimension. A proof by finite enumeration, even when exact, does not establish a theorem for arbitrary `n`. Second, the dimension transition preserves two distinct local transport types. Summing them too early hides the recurrence needed for induction. Third, the concrete graph carries finite sheet and holonomy information. These twists can glue components, change nontrivial eigenvalues, and obscure the positive sector even when they do not change maximal Perron growth.

The path to the present theorem was therefore not a direct application of a single standard comparison result. It passed through four conceptual stages.

1. **Arithmetic construction.** The n-bonacci carry graph was rebuilt from an exact number-theoretic recurrence rather than assumed from a geometric picture. This supplied dimension-parametric matrices and exact finite certificates.
2. **Nearby-family extension.** The same machinery was applied to the closely related Condition-F carry system. The attempted one-matrix generalization exposed a persistent two-color structure.
3. **Twist insight and the two-matrix breakthrough.** AM supplied the original insight that the anomalous structure should be treated as a twist, and later recognized that the same kind of twist was recurring across the broader problem. Building on that direction, Ravel made the decisive structural discovery: the system is not governed by one matrix but by two ordered generators. Ravel identified the canonical balanced/residual parent-prefix split `(Q,R)`—the "it is two matrices" step that opened the proof architecture—and showed that sheet, orientation, sign, and holonomy data are twists over that pair rather than additional base generators.
4. **General twist regime and boundary.** Finite permutation twists were seen to preserve path growth by construction, while an elementary expansive operator twist supplied a counterexample to unrestricted unweighted dominance. This suggests that the true general object is a positive scalar majorant of an operator-valued Q/R transfer system.

The mathematical novelty, if confirmed by independent literature review, lies in the synthesis: the specific canonical Q/R encoding of the n-bonacci Condition-F parent grammar; the finite noncommutative boundary substitution governing dimension growth; the universal propagation of joint comparison through that substitution; and the integration of finite twists and literal-carry bridge reductions into a proof-producing pipeline. None of Perron comparison, graph lifts, symbolic finite extensions, or reflection is novel in isolation. The claim of merit is that these established ideas lock together here into a reusable theorem for a concrete recursively defined family.

## 2. Number-theoretic origin of the n-bonacci system

### 2.1 The recurrence and carry states

Let the n-bonacci sequence be governed by the recurrence whose characteristic relation is

\[
\beta^n=\beta^{n-1}+\cdots+\beta+1.
\]

Such recurrences sit naturally in the theory of Pisot numeration and finite-state normalization [@Frougny1992; @FrougnySolomyak1992]. Rather than taking a substitution graph or a geometric contact graph as primitive, the project begins with coefficient states

\[
x_t=(a_t,a_{t+1},\ldots,a_{t+n-1})\in\mathbb Z^n
\]

and a signed carry digit

\[
d_t=-a_t+a_{t+1}+\cdots+a_{t+n}\in\{-1,0,1\}.
\]

The update is an affine integral recurrence

\[
x_{t+1}=A_nx_t+d_te_{n-1},
\]

where `A_n` is the inverse companion/carry matrix. Equivalently,

\[
a_{t+n}=a_t-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}a_{t+k}+d_t.
\]

This is a fully arithmetic definition. Vertices are integer coefficient states satisfying exact admissibility conditions; edges are installed only when the recurrence and carry constraints hold. Geometric face or parent data can be checked afterward by exact algebraic signs. This order of construction matters: it separates the finite-state number theory from any later geometric interpretation and makes the graph suitable for proof-producing computation.

The relation to Pisot numeration is not merely terminological. Pisot systems are precisely a central setting in which normalization and representation conversion become finite-state phenomena [@Frougny1992; @Frougny2002]. Condition F, introduced in the beta-expansion literature [@FrougnySolomyak1992], is also the condition used in the recent topological-group construction of Carton, Sudbery, and Yassawi [@CartonSudberyYassawi2026]. Our use of Condition F is narrower: it supplies the finite carry/prefix system whose recurrent growth is compared.

### 2.2 Universal characteristic identity

The reflective proof campaign constructs an `(n+1) x (n+1)` characteristic-matrix family and proves

\[
\det C_n(X)=X+X^2+\cdots+X^{n+1}-1
\]

for every `n >= 2`. The generated theorem is recorded as

```text
RavelGenerated.nbonacci_universal_n
```

and was accepted by the Lean kernel. The checked chain includes:

- a lower-triangular residual determinant;
- erased-index transports identifying symbolic minors;
- a residual cofactor determinant;
- the q-matrix determinant recurrence and its closed form;
- a sparse characteristic cofactor decomposition;
- polynomial normalization; and
- final theorem composition.

This theorem is deliberately stated about the generated matrix family. Identifying that family with an independently defined recurrence, substitution matrix, or transfer operator is a separate bridge obligation. The distinction is essential for both formal trust and exposition: a determinant identity can be universally proved while a semantic identification remains external unless explicitly certified.

### 2.3 Exact finite recurrent certificates

The arithmetic graph builder enumerates admissible states in a coefficient domain, installs recurrence edges, removes states unable to lie on a directed cycle, computes strongly connected components, and compares candidate growth using exact rational or integer certificates rather than floating-point eigenvalues. These calculations supplied finite-dimensional evidence and exact dominance certificates before the universal structural proof was found.

Finite certificates serve three roles in the eventual theorem:

1. they expose stable recurrent shapes and likely core components;
2. they detect failures of naive universal conjectures;
3. they produce concrete proof obligations that can be abstracted into reusable derivation operations.

They are not, however, silently promoted to an arbitrary-dimensional theorem. The project records bounded calculations, executable exact certificates, abstract paper proofs, and kernel-checked statements as different trust levels.

## 3. Extending to a near family member

The next target was not an unrelated family but a close relative: the finite Condition-F quotient associated with the same Pisot/n-bonacci grammar. This was strategically important. A close family preserves enough of the original arithmetic to reuse the proof architecture while adding precisely the feature that the first theorem had suppressed.

At the local level, every parent prefix is either empty or `[0]`. Given a transport witness `(p,q)`, define the prefix defect

\[
\delta=|q|-|p|\in\{-1,0,1\}.
\]

The exact displacement relation has the form

\[
Mx'=x+\delta e_0.
\]

The defect is dimension independent, edge local, and invariant under the quotient matching used by the implementation. It therefore gives a canonical split:

\[
Q:\delta=0,
\qquad
R:|\delta|=1.
\]

`Q` is balanced or bulk transport. `R` is one-sided or residual transport. The sign of `delta` is not a third generator; it is retained as a sheet voltage or holonomy coordinate. This distinction is the central structural discovery. It preserves the minimal noncommutative information needed by the dimension recurrence while moving finite orientation data into a twist.

Several tempting alternatives fail this invariance test. Carry-digit sign is not stable under synchronized branch subtraction. Grade is assigned only after holonomy has assembled strongly connected components. Permutation parity is not defined for arbitrary local fibres. Semantic labels such as “branch” and “transport” do not give a derived algebraic classifier. Prefix defect does.

## 4. The Q/R dominance theorem

### 4.1 Why the sum `Q+R` is not enough

For any fixed dimension, one can form the ordinary nonnegative adjacency matrix

\[
A_D=Q_D+R_D.
\]

But the transition from dimension `D` to `D+1` is not most naturally expressed as a scalar polynomial in `A_D`. Old parent roles and channels persist, while the new terminal parent contributes a finite boundary interface. Eliminating or replaying this interface produces a positive expression in ordered products of `Q_D` and `R_D`. Since `Q_D R_D` and `R_D Q_D` need not agree, summing first destroys the induction language.

The right object is therefore the free noncommutative monoid on two generators. For a word

\[
w=g_1g_2\cdots g_k,\qquad g_i\in\{Q,R\},
\]

write `w(Q,R)` for its ordered matrix product. A finite boundary substitution is a nonnegative polynomial

\[
\Phi(Q,R)=\sum_w c_w w(Q,R),\qquad c_w\in\mathbb N.
\]

Repeated words encode multiplicity. In the concrete Condition-F reduction, the terminal-parent boundary is represented by a fixed finite word polynomial generated from the parent grammar.

### 4.2 Simultaneous intertwining

Let `(Q_C,R_C)` represent a candidate component and `(Q_K,R_K)` the distinguished core pair. Suppose there is a nonnegative comparison map `P` such that

\[
Q_C P\le P Q_K,
\qquad
R_C P\le P R_K.
\]

The order is entrywise in the concrete matrix application. The abstract theorem only requires an ordered semiring in which multiplication is monotone on both sides.

Then every word satisfies

\[
w(Q_C,R_C)P\le P w(Q_K,R_K).
\]

The proof is induction on the word. For a leading `Q`, for example,

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_C w(Q_C,R_C)P
&\le Q_C P w(Q_K,R_K)\\
&\le P Q_K w(Q_K,R_K).
\end{aligned}
\]

The same argument applies to `R`. Additive monotonicity then yields

\[
\Phi(Q_C,R_C)P\le P\Phi(Q_K,R_K)
\]

for every finite nonnegative word polynomial `Phi`.

These two abstract results are kernel checked in Lean as `qr_word_intertwiner` and `qr_polynomial_intertwiner`. Their significance is larger than their short proofs might suggest. They are the algebraic hinge that lets a locally verified two-generator comparison survive the entire dimension-dependent boundary construction.

### 4.3 Dimension induction and final sandwich

At the minimal alphabet `D=2`, the recurrent parent-role graph consists of the predicted core itself, and the simultaneous comparison is the identity. Under `D -> D+1`, old roles and channels persist. Every new role lies in the finite-depth terminal-parent boundary. The same nonnegative Q/R word polynomial is applied to the full quotient pair and to the transported core pair. Hence the joint comparison propagates dimension by dimension.

The abstract natural-number induction interface is kernel checked as `jointQR_dimension_induction`. After the concrete quotient and literal carry system are connected by executable bridge certificates, the final comparison takes the form

\[
L_D\le H_D\le K_D\le L_D,
\]

where `L_D` is the literal carry object, `H_D` the finite quotient or assembled comparison object, and `K_D` the predicted core. Antisymmetry gives equality at the relevant ordered invariant. The abstract closure is kernel checked as `universal_dominance_sandwich`.

The paper must be precise about what is formalized at which layer. Lean checks the order-theoretic word propagation, polynomial propagation, induction schema, and sandwich. C++ derivations certify that the generated concrete boundary and quotient objects satisfy the hypotheses. A complete archival supplement should therefore ship both the Lean source and the exact bridge-certificate manifests.

### 4.4 Spectral consequence

For nonnegative matrices, entrywise comparison propagates to powers and spectral radii [@BermanPlemmons1994; @Seneta2006]. Intertwining comparisons can be converted into Perron bounds under the positivity hypotheses used by the concrete certificates. The resulting conclusion is that the distinguished core is spectrally maximal in the untwisted finite Condition-F quotient. The terminal and branching reductions then transport this result to the literal carry system.

This is not simply the observation that `rho(A) <= rho(B)` whenever `A <= B`. The theorem identifies the correct two-generator structure whose comparison survives a recursively generated noncommutative boundary substitution. The ordinary Perron comparison is the closure mechanism; the Q/R grammar is the family-specific content.

## 5. Twists as finite extensions

### 5.1 Channelized permutation lifts

The concrete Condition-F quotient is a directed multigraph. Distinct component channels between the same ordered pair of quotient roles remain parallel edges. Each channel carries a permutation of a finite sheet fibre. The lifted graph is therefore a permutation skew product.

For every base path and every chosen initial fibre state, there is exactly one lifted path. Consequently, row path counts in the lift agree with those of the scalar channel-count base after summing over terminal fibre states. At the Perron-growth level, the finite fibre contributes no exponential factor. This is the finite-extension or zero-fibre-entropy intuition familiar from symbolic dynamics [@LindMarcus1995; @LagariasWang2014].

Voltage and gain graph theory gives a complementary spectral description. A finite group voltage assignment decomposes the lift into representation sectors, with the trivial representation recovering the positive channel-count matrix and nontrivial representations carrying phase or matrix coefficients [@DalfFiolSiran2018; @DalfoEtAl2019]. Related lift literature studies how large the nontrivial eigenvalues can be [@BiluLinial2006; @MarcusSpielmanSrivastava2015]. Our use is different: the positive trivial sector controls the Perron growth of the finite permutation lift.

### 5.2 Two-sheet Fourier form

For an orientation double cover, balanced channels preserve the sheet and residual channels flip it. The lifted adjacency is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
Q&R\\
R&Q
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The even/odd Fourier transform gives the sectors

\[
Q+R,\qquad Q-R.
\]

Since

\[
|Q-R|\le Q+R
\]

entrywise, the nontrivial sector is spectrally dominated by the positive sector. This is the simplest visible instance of the twist principle.

For larger finite fibres, irreducible representations replace the two characters. The trivial representation again gives the channel-count pair. Nontrivial sectors have matrix coefficients bounded in modulus or operator norm by the positive channel counts. The result can also be seen before representation decomposition through unique path lifting.

### 5.3 Holonomy gluing

Twists are not irrelevant to component structure. A nontrivial holonomy group can glue several untwisted sheet components into a single recurrent component. Therefore component classification must occur after lift assembly, not before. The project’s twisted quotient operation reconstructs the exact concrete edge multiset from regular bipartite sheet relations, decomposes each relation into perfect matchings, and records each matching as one parallel permutation edge. It then computes strongly connected components in the assembled lift.

This resolves an apparent tension. Twists may change which vertices belong to the same recurrent component and may create substantial nontrivial spectrum, yet finite permutation twists still do not create a larger Perron growth rate than the positive base sector. They determine assembly before one of the existing terminal, pure-grade, or mixed-grade cases is selected; they do not create a fourth asymptotic growth mechanism.

### 5.4 Gauge changes and products

A vertexwise fibre relabelling replaces each edge permutation `p_e:u->v` by

\[
p'_e=h_vp_eh_u^{-1}.
\]

The lifted matrices are conjugate by a block-diagonal permutation matrix. Hence their characteristic polynomials, spectra, path counts, and dominance conclusions agree.

Two finite twists combine on the product fibre by

\[
(a,b)\mapsto(p_e(a),q_e(b)).
\]

This is again a finite permutation skew product. Thus phase, orientation, finite memory, and several holonomy coordinates may be combined without leaving the theorem’s regime.

## 6. The counterexample and the natural boundary

The finite-twist theorem might tempt one to state an unrestricted principle: any operator-valued decoration of the base is dominated by the unweighted channel-count matrix. This is false.

Take the graph with one vertex and one loop. The unweighted base is

\[
B_{\mathrm{count}}=[1].
\]

Decorate the loop by the scalar fibre operator

\[
U=[2].
\]

Then the twisted transfer operator is `[2]`, and

\[
\rho([2])=2>1=\rho([1]).
\]

The example is minimal but decisive. Finite permutation and unitary twists contribute no exponential fibre expansion. An arbitrary operator cocycle can. The unweighted theorem’s natural hypothesis is therefore contractivity, isometry, or finite permutation action—not “twist” in complete generality.

This boundary aligns with the broader theory of transfer-operator and matrix cocycles, where base complexity and fibre Lyapunov growth jointly determine asymptotic expansion [@GundlachLatushkin2003; @FroylandLloydQuas2008; @BlumenthalMorris2018]. It also connects the Q/R formulation to the joint spectral radius, the asymptotic growth rate of noncommutative matrix products [@RotaStrang1960; @BergerWang1992; @Jungers2009].

## 7. Proposed norm-weighted Q/R dominance

The counterexample does not merely negate a generalization. It points to the corrected theorem.

Let each base channel `e:u->v` carry an operator `U_e` between finite-dimensional normed fibres. Choose a certified scalar bound

\[
\|U_e\|\le a_e,
\qquad a_e\ge0.
\]

Define the positive scalar majorant

\[
A(v,u)=\sum_{e:u\to v}m_ea_e,
\]

where `m_e` records channel multiplicity or a nonnegative scalar weight. For a length-`k` path

\[
\gamma=e_k\cdots e_1,
\]

submultiplicativity gives

\[
\|U_\gamma\|
=\|U_{e_k}\cdots U_{e_1}\|
\le\prod_{i=1}^k a_{e_i}.
\]

After summing paths,

\[
\|(T^k)_{vu}\|\le(A^k)_{vu}.
\]

A compatible monotone block norm then yields

\[
\|T^k\|\le\|A^k\|,
\]

and Gelfand’s formula gives

\[
\rho(T)\le\rho(A).
\]

For permutation, unitary, or contractive twists one may choose `a_e <= 1`, so `A` is bounded by the ordinary channel-count adjacency and the existing dominance theorem applies. For the scalar loop `[2]`, the valid majorant is `[2]`, not `[1]`, and equality is recovered.

The Q/R version assigns separate positive majorants `Q^#` and `R^#` to balanced and residual channels. Every operator-valued Q/R word is norm dominated by the corresponding scalar word, and every nonnegative boundary polynomial remains dominated. Thus the already kernel-checked word-polynomial induction is expected to survive after replacing channel counts by certified operator-norm weights.

This section is a theorem target, not yet part of the same formal status as the finite permutation theorem. The current repository contains the counterexample and the conceptual majorant, but the reusable engine operation must still construct operator/path certificates rather than accepting user-supplied norm labels and asserting the conclusion.

## 8. Proof-producing reflection in C++ and Lean

### 8.1 Architecture

The implementation follows a deliberately asymmetric trust design:

```text
Lua activation
  -> ordinary exact C++ math-library execution
  -> typed semantic reflection trace
  -> generated proof campaign
  -> reusable derivation operations
  -> dependency-closed proof artifacts
  -> Lean serialization
  -> Lean kernel check
```

Lua selects or configures a theorem campaign. It does not supply proof text. Ordinary exact mathematical operations execute in C++ using arbitrary-precision integers, rationals, symbolic polynomials, and exact matrices. When reflection is active, those operations emit typed semantic nodes and provenance edges into a trace.

A campaign generator maps the observed trace into dependency-linked tasks. A generic campaign executor repeatedly applies installed derivation operations. Operations include triangular determinant closure, erased-index transport, sparse cofactor recurrence, recurrence solving, polynomial normalization, pair-word propagation, boundary-polynomial closure, and final theorem composition. An unsupported derivation remains a typed block. It is not repaired by inserting raw Lean text.

Only closed artifacts are serialized. The emitted Lean module contains definitions and theorems whose dependencies are explicit. Lean then checks the result. This use of an untrusted or partially trusted generator with a small trusted checker is consonant with proof-producing and reflective theorem-proving practice [@Harrison2006; @EbnerEtAl2017; @deMouraUllrich2021], but the particular reflection source is ordinary exact mathematical code rather than a standalone tactic script or external SAT certificate.

### 8.2 “Proven reflection of the code”

The intended phrase requires care. The C++ executable is not itself absorbed wholesale into Lean’s trusted kernel. Instead, proof-relevant mathematical operations are instrumented so that each produces a semantic claim together with exact operands, results, and provenance. The proof campaign reconstructs a derivation using operations whose Lean renderings are independently checkable.

Thus the trust chain is:

1. **Observation:** exact code reports what operation it performed.
2. **Validation:** the C++ derivation operation checks local structural premises and produces a typed certificate.
3. **Composition:** the campaign closes dependencies and forbids open goals from serialization.
4. **Formal replay:** Lean receives definitions and proofs corresponding to the certified stages.
5. **Kernel acceptance:** the Lean kernel determines whether the final theorem follows.

The phrase “checking each stage” therefore refers to two complementary checks. The engine checks type, dependency, exact-data, and certificate invariants at every stage. Lean checks the mathematical proof term of the exported theorem. A successful C++ run is not reported as a kernel proof; a kernel-checked theorem is tied to the exact emitted source and hash.

### 8.3 Why the architecture mattered mathematically

The universal n-bonacci proof initially failed at a family-specific seam: symbolic deletion of rows and columns from an `n`-dependent matrix. Finite matrices hid this issue because their determinants could simply be evaluated. The engine was extended with reusable erased-index transport and sparse cofactor operations. Once those operations existed, the universal determinant theorem became an ordinary campaign instance.

The Q/R theorem repeated the pattern with a twist. The missing operation was no longer matrix-minor transport but noncommutative pair-word transport. Implementing `derive_condition_f_joint_pair_comparison` and the associated word-polynomial closure turned a family-specific proof idea into reusable engine capability. This is the project’s governing engineering rule: when a derivation is missing, implement the derivation operation rather than hard-code the resulting proof.

## 9. Relationship to existing literature

### 9.1 Perron--Frobenius comparison

The scalar closure rests on classical nonnegative matrix theory [@BermanPlemmons1994; @Seneta2006; @HornJohnson2013]. Entrywise modulus domination also gives the standard estimate

\[
\rho(T)\le\rho(|T|)\le\rho(B)
\]

when `|T| <= B`. Our claim is not novelty for this inequality. The new content is the identification of a recursively preserved Q/R majorant and the proof that the concrete boundary extension is a positive noncommutative polynomial in those generators.

### 9.2 Pisot numeration and Condition F

Finite-state normalization in Pisot bases and linear numeration systems is classical [@Frougny1992; @FrougnySolomyak1992; @Frougny2002]. Recent work of Carton, Sudbery, and Yassawi treats Pisot numerations preserving zeros and explicitly relates this to Condition F [@CartonSudberyYassawi2026]. Rauzy-fractal and substitution frameworks provide the broader geometric context [@ArnouxIto2001]. The present theorem concerns maximal recurrent growth in a particular carry/parent transfer system, not the general existence of finite automata or the general topology of Pisot numeration groups.

### 9.3 Graph lifts and representation sectors

Voltage-graph methods decompose finite lifts through group representations [@DalfFiolSiran2018; @DalfoEtAl2019]. Two-lift and Ramanujan-lift work studies nontrivial spectra [@BiluLinial2006; @MarcusSpielmanSrivastava2015]. We use the same structural language but ask a different question: why the trivial positive sector remains Perron maximal in the specific n-bonacci/Condition-F transfer family, and how that statement interacts with a dimension-recursive Q/R boundary polynomial.

### 9.4 Symbolic extensions

Finite labelled graphs and right-resolving presentations connect path growth, entropy, and spectral radius [@Lind1984; @LindMarcus1995; @LagariasWang2014]. A finite permutation fibre is a zero-exponential-growth extension, which explains at an entropy level why unique path lifting preserves the base growth rate. The project requires a stronger constructive statement because the exact quotient, channel multiplicities, and holonomy assembly are inputs to subsequent dominance certificates.

### 9.5 Cocycles and joint spectral growth

Operator-valued twists belong naturally to cocycle and transfer-operator theory [@GundlachLatushkin2003; @FroylandLloydQuas2008; @BlumenthalMorris2018]. The Q/R grammar makes the joint-spectral aspect explicit: asymptotic growth depends on ordered products of two generators [@RotaStrang1960; @BergerWang1992; @Jungers2009]. The finite-permutation theorem occupies the zero-fibre-growth regime. The proposed norm-weighted theorem adds the fibre growth back as a positive potential.

## 11. Discussion and future work

The counterexample changes the direction of generalization. Before it, “twist invariance” could be mistaken for a statement about all fibre decorations. After it, the theorem has a clearer shape:

> The positive Q/R parent system dominates every twist whose fibre action is bounded by the chosen positive scalar channel majorant.

Finite permutation and unitary twists are the simplest case because the unweighted count is already a valid majorant. Expansive twists require a weighted one.

Several questions now become natural.

1. **Sharp positive majorants.** Edgewise operator norms are easy but may be crude. Can one use invariant cone norms, representation-specific bounds, or block scaling to produce a smaller Q/R majorant?
2. **Joint spectral equality.** When does the twisted cocycle attain the scalar majorant growth? The one-loop example does, while cancellation in phase sectors can make the inequality strict.
3. **Boundary substitution with weights.** Does the concrete terminal-parent polynomial preserve optimal weights, or only valid upper bounds?
4. **Infinite-dimensional fibres.** The pathwise argument extends formally to bounded operators on Banach fibres, but compactness and spectral-radius closure require careful hypotheses.
