To this day, the frame set has been determined for only a few classes of functions.
Of particular importance was an influential conjecture of Daubechies saying that if
both g and its Fourier transform are positive, then the Gabor system G(g, α, β) is a
frame whenever αβ < 1 [6, p. 981]. In other words, the frame set is maximal, and
the necessary condition (1.1) is also suﬀicient. Although this conjecture was shown
to be false in general by Janssen [21], it initiated extensive research on identifying
function classes where the frame set equals the maximal set {(α, β) ∈ R2+ : αβ < 1},
which we will abbreviate by M = {(α, β) ∈ R2+ : αβ < 1} [5, 13, 17, 19, 22, 23].
1.2. Totally positive functions. An important step towards the deeper understanding of the frame set was the insight from [19] that the appropriate notion of
positivity in the study of Gabor frames was total positivity of functions. A function
g : R → R is called totally positive if it is not constant and for every n ∈ N and
every x1 < · · · < xn and y1 < · · · < yn , the determinant of the matrix M ∈ Rn×n
with entries Mjk = g(xj − yk ) satisfies det M ≥ 0. Every integrable totally positive
function has exponential decay [38]. Assuming also continuity, their frame set is
then automatically contained in the open set M.
Totally positive functions occur in many branches of mathematics, e.g., spline
theory, statistics, the representation of infinite-dimensional groups, and also in
sampling theory and time-frequency analysis. General references are the books by
Karlin [27] and Pinkus [36], and a survey of their role in time-frequency analysis
and sampling is contained in [12].
1.3. Main result. The study of frame sets for totally positive functions has been
the subject of a series of papers [13, 17, 19, 22, 34, 42] and draws on techniques from
several areas of mathematics, including harmonic analysis, functional analysis, and
complex analysis. Gabor systems associated with totally positive functions were
also investigated in several related contexts [2,9,15,31], including discrete settings,
completeness problems, algorithmic aspects, and quantitative estimates for frame
bounds. Central to this line of research is the following conjecture in [11, 19]: for
every continuous totally positive function in L1 (R), the frame set coincides with
M = (α, β) ∈ R2+ : αβ < 1 . This statement is often referred to as the frame set
conjecture for totally positive functions.

In this paper, we prove the frame set conjecture for totally positive functions in

Theorem 1.1. The frame set of every continuous totally positive function g ∈

required to prove injectivity. Dealing with the kernel and cokernel of an operator

techniques will be useful for other problems in time-frequency analysis and we
have therefore tried to make the presentation as transparent as possible.

3. Operator theory tools
The operator-theoretic argument that will give injectivity of the pre-Gramians

self-similar Fredholm operator is injective. Together, these results strengthen the
surjectivity statement obtained in the previous section to the invertibility required
by Proposition 2.2.

6.1. Formalization of the main result. Only a small amount of Lean syntax
is needed to read the formalization of the above theorem. A general definition in

theorem. In a statement, the hypotheses are listed after the name of the theorem,

The parameters α and β are declared as real numbers by {α β : ℝ}; the curly
braces mark them as implicit arguments, whose values Lean infers automatically

declared by (g : ℝ → ℝ), and the hypothesis hyp4 combines the properties of g

predicates IsTPIntegrableContinuous and IsGaborFrame are defined in the following

6.2. The hypotheses on the window. The notion of total positivity is formalized directly from its definition in terms of determinants.
