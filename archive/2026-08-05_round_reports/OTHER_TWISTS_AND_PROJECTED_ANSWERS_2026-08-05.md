# Other twists of n-bonacci and immediately projected conclusions

The Condition-F construction is a finite cocycle/groupoid extension of an untwisted two-generator transfer system `(Q,R)`.  The same root theorem applies to several other twists.

## Finite permutation or voltage twists
Attach a finite permutation to each Q/R channel.  Every colored base path lifts uniquely from each initial fibre point.  The trivial representation recovers the untwisted channel-count pair, and all other finite-group sectors are dominated by it.

Immediate projections: unchanged exponential path growth and topological entropy; spectrum decomposes by irreducible fibre representations; holonomy controls component splitting and periods.

## Signed and phase twists
Weight channels by signs or unit complex phases.  These are one-dimensional unitary representations of the voltage group.

Immediate projections: twisted spectral radius is bounded by the untwisted Perron radius; cohomologous phase assignments are conjugate; cancellations may change individual eigenvalues and correlations but cannot increase maximal growth.

## Matrix-unitary twists
Replace scalar phases by finite-dimensional unitary representations.

Immediate projections: the lift decomposes into represented transfer matrices; every sector is bounded by the trivial positive sector.  This covers nonabelian finite holonomy and locally varying sheet permutations.

## Groupoid/local-fibre twists
Allow fibre size and isotropy to vary with the role.  The transfer operator lives in a finite groupoid algebra rather than one group algebra.

Immediate projections: the augmentation remains the untwisted Q/R pair; representation sectors describe local holonomy; Perron maximality remains in the positive trivial module when local channel maps are permutation-regular.

## Coboundary or gauge twists
Change voltages by endpoint gauges.  This conjugates the represented transfer operators.

Immediate projections: spectrum, growth, entropy, and dominance are unchanged.  Many apparent phase conventions are therefore presentation choices rather than new dynamics.

## Finite-memory / higher-block twists
Make the voltage depend on a bounded history of Q/R channels.  Passing to a higher-block presentation turns it into an ordinary finite-state cocycle.

Immediate projections: the same extension theorem applies after state expansion; entropy is unchanged by the conjugate higher-block presentation; phase-dependent recurrence questions become finite transfer-matrix questions.

## Affine or translational twists
Attach a finite or zero-entropy translation fibre rather than only a permutation fibre.

Immediate projections: when the fibre action is finite-to-one or has zero entropy, topological entropy remains that of the base.  Exact spectral-radius equality requires an additional bounded/unitary or block-triangular hypothesis; it is not automatic for expanding affine fibres.

## Random or probabilistic twists
Choose channel voltages randomly or according to a stationary process.

Immediate projections: the deterministic Perron equality becomes a Lyapunov-exponent or pressure question.  The untwisted matrix still supplies a norm bound, but exact equality generally requires symmetry or amenability assumptions.

## Exterior, symmetric, and tensor-power twists
Apply Q/R simultaneously to tuples, multisets, or alternating tensors of paths.

Immediate projections: these encode higher-order correlations, multi-particle/grade dynamics, and subdominant Lyapunov exponents.  They are not generally finite permutation extensions of the base, so dominance must be checked through compound-cocycle inequalities rather than assumed.

## Questions that project immediately
For every genuine finite permutation/unitary cocycle extension:

- maximal path-growth rate and topological entropy come from the untwisted sector;
- the full spectrum splits into representation sectors;
- component periods and gluing are computed from holonomy;
- cohomologous twists have conjugate operators;
- periodic lifts are classified by the voltage accumulated around base cycles;
- the n-bonacci dominance theorem extends unchanged whenever the new twist is finite, locally permutation-regular, and leaves the same Q/R augmentation.

The dividing line is simple: finite/unitary twists modify representation sectors but not Perron growth; expanding or non-finite-to-one fibres can introduce genuinely new growth and require a new theorem.
