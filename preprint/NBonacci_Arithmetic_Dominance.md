# Arithmetic carry blocks and finite certificates for n-bonacci dominance

**RavelMathPub preprint draft — 2 August 2026**  
**Status:** exact algebra and finite certificates are proved or replayable;
the uniform periodic-carry escape lemma remains open.

## Abstract

We describe an arithmetic route to dominance questions for the n-bonacci
contact graph.  Instead of constructing the paper corona first, we enumerate
the exact coefficient hull, install the carry transitions, and isolate its
recurrent components.  The central algebraic observation is that the inverse
carry matrix (A) satisfies

\[
A+A^2+\cdots+A^n=I,
\qquad A^{n+1}=2A-I.
\]

Consequently an ((n+1))-step block is a linear map with a forcing term
depending only on (n+1) signed carry digits.  We formalize this identity,
bound the forcing term, and provide replayable integer chamber certificates
for strict escape in bounded boxes.  Exact hull computations find a unique
dominant recurrent component for (3\le n\le8), including components absent
from the smaller paper corona.  These results establish a robust finite
certificate pipeline and narrow the remaining universal statement to one
explicit lemma: every periodic signed-carry word has ternary coefficients.
We do not claim that universal lemma here.

## 1. The arithmetic model

Write a coefficient state as

\[
x_t=(a_t,a_{t+1},\ldots,a_{t+n-1})\in\mathbb Z^n.
\]

The signed carry at time (t) is

\[
d_t=-a_t+a_{t+1}+\cdots+a_{t+n},
\qquad d_t\in\{-1,0,1\},
\]

and the state update is

\[
x_{t+1}=Ax_t+d_t e_{n-1}.
\]

Here (A) is the inverse companion/carry matrix.  All transitions in the
arithmetic hull are generated by this integer recurrence; face inequalities
and parent labels are checked separately by exact algebraic signs.

### The machine hidden in the implementation

The C++ cycle probe is not an opaque graph search.  Its `tail` line is the
recurrence itself:

\[
 a_{t+n}=a_t-
       \sum_{k=1}^{n-1}a_{t+k}+d_t.
\]

If (S_t=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{t+k}) is the window sum, every installed edge
satisfies the scalar projection

\[
 S_{t+1}=a_t+d_t,
 \qquad |S_{t+1}-a_t|\le1.
\]

Thus the high-dimensional machine is a shift register driven by a ternary
error, with the window sum as a one-step delayed observation of its first
coordinate.  The cycle-pruning queue computes the greatest subgraph on which
this driven shift can run forever.  In every tested box, the survivors are
already ternary; the unresolved proof is precisely the assertion that this
finite-core phenomenon persists without a box bound.

The scalar identity is now kernel checked as `carryUpdate_sum` and
`carryUpdate_sum_digit_bound` in the Lean catalogue.

## 2. The block identity

The characteristic polynomial of (A) is

\[
X^n-X^{n-1}-\cdots-X-1.
\]

The resulting geometric-sum identity is kernel checked in
`lean/nbonacci_margin_catalogue.lean`:

\[
\sum_{r=1}^{n}A^r=I.
\]

Multiplying by (A-I) gives the equivalent block relation

\[
A^{n+1}=2A-I.
\]

Iterating the affine recurrence over one block gives

\[
x_{t+n+1}=A^{n+1}x_t+F_t
        =(2A-I)x_t+F_t,
\]

where

\[
F_t=\sum_{r=0}^{n}d_{t+r}A^{n-r}e_{n-1}.
\]

The Lean development expands this forcing vector coordinatewise.  If every
carry digit is in ({-1,0,1}), then each coordinate satisfies

\[
|F_{t,i}|\le4,
\qquad \sum_i|F_{t,i}|\le4n.
\]

The C++ regression checks the same identities exactly for every dimension
(2\le n\le40), independently of Lean.

## 3. Finite recurrent dominance

The arithmetic-hull builder enumerates a coefficient box, filters exact
face-valid states, installs all carry transitions, and removes states that
cannot lie on a directed cycle.  Recurrent components are compared with
integer/rational Perron certificates rather than floating eigenvalues.

For the box ([-1,1]^n), the computation finds one dominant recurrent
component for every (3\le n\le8).  The hull is an over-approximation of the
paper corona, so dominance in this larger graph is the stronger finite test.

| (n) | hull states in exact window | cyclic SCCs | core nodes | core Perron root (diagnostic) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 108 | 1 | 14 | 1.395337 |
| 4 | 548 | 3 | 46 | 1.623354 |
| 5 | 2,390 | 3 | 108 | 1.756228 |
| 6 | 9,594 | 7 | 210 | 1.836484 |
| 7 | 36,666 | 7 | 362 | 1.886782 |
| 8 | 135,416 | 17 | 574 | 1.919376 |

The extra hull states matter: at (n=6), the largest competitor has 236
nodes while the known corona component has 221.  It is nevertheless strictly
dominated.  Enlarging the coefficient box to ([-2,2]^n) for (3\le n\le5)
produces many transient states but no new cyclic translation.

## 4. Chamber certificates for the escape step

To attack the missing periodic-carry lemma, we quotient the transient carry
graph by finite sign/magnitude chambers.  A certificate records:

1. chamber names and every surviving transition;
2. the weighted difference constraint for a proposed rank;
3. integer offsets produced by Bellman–Ford;
4. an exact replay check for every edge.

The checker also enforces the involution (x\mapsto-x): every chamber has a
sign-negated partner with equal offset.  Certificates use at most the
configured 10 GiB virtual-memory cap and are replay-only after generation.
The finite-cycle contradiction used to interpret a strict rank is
kernel-checked as `no_strict_rank_finite`; the closed-exterior specialization
is `no_strict_rank_closed_subset`.

Representative results for the `gaps-mod` chamber grammar are:

| (n), bound | chambers | weighted edges | replay |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 3, 8 | 4,388 | 9,008 | pass |
| 4, 8 | 82,194 | 149,778 | pass |
| 5, 6 | 371,198 | 611,658 | pass |
| 6, 4 | 531,186 | 812,926 | pass |
| 7, 3 | 822,844 | 1,181,154 | pass |
| 8, 2 | 388,678 | 523,824 | pass |

The rank-feature search now tests sign, gap-linear, quadratic, and richer
low-degree feature families directly against the edge inequalities.  All four
families are infeasible on the (n=3), bound-8 certificate.  This is a useful
negative result: the universal rank is not captured by one of these naïve
linear formulas.  Comparing bounds 8 and 12 shows that every smaller chamber
persists and every smaller edge remains valid, but the canonical offsets are
not translation-equivalent.  Box-specific longest-path offsets therefore
cannot be promoted by simply taking a limit.

This normalization issue is not itself an obstruction.  The merge tool
`python/nbonacci_chamber_merge.py` identifies chambers symbolically and unions
the two finite constraint systems; the n=3, bounds 8 and 12 systems admit one
common integer potential.  Cross-box merging is therefore the preferred
promotion test, while a genuine positive difference cycle is a sound failure.
The same potential merges bounds 8, 12, 16, and 32 (98,996 chambers and
285,872 weighted edges at bound 32), despite a new 162-node quotient SCC
appearing at bound 16.  The merge solver propagates constraints componentwise
over the quotient SCC DAG rather than performing a global quadratic relaxation.
The same cross-box merge passes for (n=4), bounds 8 and 10, over 184,450
chambers and 349,088 weighted edges. Extending to bound 12 gives 352,178
chambers and 701,448 weighted edges, again with one shared potential.
The existing certificates merge similarly at (n=5), bounds 4 and 6
(371,198 chambers and 611,658 edges), and at (n=6), bounds 3 and 4
(531,186 chambers and 812,926 edges). These are cross-box promotion tests,
not yet a uniform-in-(n) theorem.

An abstraction audit is informative: sign-only, ordered-magnitude, and
gap-capped quotients fail their strict-rank tests, whereas exact gaps pass.
For n=3, bounds 8 and 12, the residue-free exact-gap certificates merge over
4,964 chambers and 14,302 edges. The eventual template must therefore retain
unbounded gap data rather than only a finite sign summary.

The first positive symbolic synthesis is a `sector-gaps` rank: one affine
function per sign sector, with integer coefficients on the exact gap
coordinates. It passes the merged (n=3), bounds 8 and 12 certificate, and
also the merged bounds 8, 12, 16, and 32 system. The companion replay checker
uses integer arithmetic only. This is the current candidate template for a
uniform proof, not yet a dimension-parametric theorem.

## 5. What is proved and what remains

The following are proved or exactly replayable:

- the characteristic-polynomial and ((n+1))-block identities;
- the explicit forcing formula and its coarse coordinate/(L^1) bounds;
- finite arithmetic-hull recurrence and Perron dominance for the dimensions
  listed above;
- finite chamber rank certificates, including sign symmetry and replay.

The remaining universal lemma is:

> If (a_t) is periodic and
> (d_t=-a_t+a_{t+1}+\cdots+a_{t+n}\in\{-1,0,1\}) for every (t), then
> (a_t\in\{-1,0,1\}) for every (t).

The block identity reduces this to a strict escape argument for the exterior
chambers, followed by the elementary fact that a strict integer rank cannot
increase around a finite directed cycle.  The current certificates establish
the finite half of that plan but do not yet supply a dimension- and
box-independent rank formula.

## 6. Reproducibility

From the project root:

```sh
make nbonacci_block_identity_test
make nbonacci_block_forcing_probe
make nbonacci_chamber_stability
make nbonacci_rank_feature_search
make lean-check
```

The generated JSON certificates can be independently replayed:

```sh
python3 python/nbonacci_chamber_certificate_check.py \
  /tmp/n3_b8_rank.json
```

The authoritative theorem boundary is maintained in
`docs/THEOREM_STATUS.md`; this draft intentionally uses “finite certificate”
where the universal escape lemma is still open.
